ABSTRACT
Artificial Intelligence (AI) has transformed the recruitment industry by enabling organizations to automate various stages of the hiring process. However, many existing AI interview platforms primarily rely on text-based interactions or predefined question sets, limiting their ability to conduct realistic interviews, evaluate communication skills effectively, and adapt dynamically to candidate responses. Furthermore, most available solutions lack real-time conversational capabilities, intelligent follow-up questioning, behavioral assessment, and comprehensive interview analytics, making them less suitable for practical recruitment scenarios.
To address these limitations, this project presents an AI-Driven Real-Time Interview Platform capable of conducting adaptive voice-based technical interviews for software and Artificial Intelligence-related roles. The proposed system integrates Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), Large Language Models (LLMs), Text-to-Speech (TTS), Voice Activity Detection (VAD), and an adaptive dialogue management framework to simulate the behavior of a professional human interviewer. Unlike conventional interview systems, the proposed platform continuously analyzes candidate responses, generates context-aware follow-up questions, evaluates technical knowledge and communication skills, prevents repetitive questioning, and dynamically adjusts the interview flow based on candidate performance.
The system adopts a modular architecture consisting of a browser-based client interface, a FastAPI backend, a real-time voice communication pipeline, an intelligent dialogue management engine, an evaluation module, and an automated reporting subsystem. Speech input from candidates is transcribed using Deepgram Speech-to-Text services, while conversational intelligence is powered by the Groq-hosted Llama 3.3 language model. Interview responses are synthesized into natural speech using Cartesia Text-to-Speech, enabling seamless two-way voice communication with minimal latency. Supporting components including transcript processing, silence detection, interruption handling, semantic duplicate prevention, adaptive evaluation, and coverage tracking collectively enhance interview realism and user experience.
The proposed platform was designed and implemented using Python, FastAPI, Pipecat, WebSockets, JavaScript, HTML, CSS, and modern AI APIs. The developed system supports structured technical interviews, intelligent domain progression, automated candidate evaluation, recruiter-oriented reporting, and real-time conversational interaction. Experimental testing demonstrated stable real-time communication, low-latency response generation, effective adaptive questioning, and consistent interview flow management across multiple interview sessions. The modular architecture also enables future integration of facial behavior analysis, emotion recognition, multilingual interviewing, and cloud-based deployment for enterprise-scale recruitment.
This project contributes a comprehensive AI-powered interviewing platform that bridges the gap between traditional online assessments and human-conducted interviews by combining advanced speech technologies, natural language understanding, adaptive decision-making, and automated evaluation into a unified intelligent recruitment system. The proposed solution provides an efficient, scalable, and extensible framework for conducting realistic technical interviews while reducing interviewer workload, improving candidate assessment consistency, and supporting data-driven hiring decisions.

CHAPTER 1
Introduction
  INTRODUCTION
The rapid advancement of Artificial Intelligence (AI) has transformed numerous industries by automating complex tasks, improving decision-making processes, and enhancing user experiences. Among these industries, recruitment and talent acquisition have experienced significant technological evolution through the adoption of AI-powered tools for resume screening, candidate ranking, online assessments, and interview scheduling. Organizations increasingly rely on intelligent recruitment systems to reduce hiring time, minimize operational costs, and improve the overall quality of candidate selection. Despite these advancements, one of the most critical stages of the recruitment process—the technical interview—still largely depends on human interviewers, making it time-consuming, expensive, and susceptible to inconsistency and subjective bias.
Traditional online interviewing platforms primarily focus on scheduling interviews or facilitating video communication between interviewers and candidates. Although these platforms simplify remote interviewing, they do not actively participate in evaluating candidate performance or adapting interview questions based on candidate responses. Similarly, many AI-based interviewing solutions currently available rely on scripted conversations, static questionnaires, or text-based chatbots that lack the ability to conduct realistic, dynamic, and context-aware interviews. As a result, these systems often fail to accurately assess a candidate's technical knowledge, communication skills, analytical thinking, and problem-solving abilities in a manner comparable to an experienced human interviewer.
Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs), Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), Text-to-Speech (TTS), and real-time conversational AI have created new opportunities for developing intelligent interview systems capable of conducting interactive voice-based interviews. These technologies enable machines to understand spoken language, generate natural responses, maintain conversational context, and produce human-like speech with remarkably low latency. By integrating these technologies into a unified architecture, AI systems can move beyond simple question-answer interactions and provide adaptive interviewing experiences that closely resemble real-world technical interviews.
The development of intelligent interview platforms has become particularly important due to the increasing demand for remote hiring and global recruitment. Companies frequently receive hundreds or even thousands of applications for a single position, making manual interviewing an increasingly difficult and resource-intensive process. Recruiters and technical interviewers must invest considerable time evaluating each candidate while ensuring fairness and consistency across interviews. Human interviewers may unintentionally introduce bias, vary the difficulty of questions, overlook important competencies, or provide inconsistent evaluations depending on experience, workload, or personal judgment. An AI-powered interviewing system can help address these challenges by providing standardized interview procedures, objective evaluation criteria, and consistent questioning strategies for every candidate.
The proposed AI-Driven Real-Time Interview Platform is designed to simulate the behavior of a professional technical interviewer by combining state-of-the-art speech processing, natural language understanding, adaptive dialogue management, and automated evaluation techniques. Unlike conventional chatbot-based interview systems, the proposed platform conducts interviews entirely through natural voice conversations, allowing candidates to interact with the system as they would with a human interviewer. The platform continuously listens to candidate responses, converts speech into text using Automatic Speech Recognition, evaluates responses using Large Language Models, generates context-aware follow-up questions, synthesizes responses into natural speech using Text-to-Speech technology, and produces comprehensive interview reports for recruiters upon interview completion.
To achieve real-time performance, the system adopts a modular architecture consisting of multiple interconnected components. The browser-based client captures candidate audio and communicates with the backend through WebSocket connections to support low-latency streaming. The backend processes incoming audio using Voice Activity Detection (VAD) to distinguish speech from silence and forwards speech segments to the Speech-to-Text service for transcription. Once the candidate's response has been transcribed, an intelligent dialogue management engine evaluates the answer, determines the next interview action, selects appropriate technical questions, and generates interviewer responses using a Large Language Model. Finally, the generated response is converted back into speech using a Text-to-Speech engine and delivered to the candidate, completing a continuous conversational cycle. This pipeline enables natural interaction without requiring the candidate to type responses or manually control the interview flow.
A distinguishing feature of the proposed platform is its adaptive dialogue management capability. Instead of asking a predefined sequence of questions, the system analyzes candidate responses in real time and dynamically determines whether additional probing questions are required, whether the candidate has sufficiently demonstrated competency in a particular domain, or whether the interview should progress to another technical topic. This adaptive interviewing strategy enables more accurate assessment of candidate knowledge while maintaining a conversational flow similar to that of experienced human interviewers. The platform also incorporates mechanisms to prevent repetitive questioning, manage interview coverage across multiple technical domains, and maintain contextual awareness throughout the interview session.
In addition to intelligent questioning, the proposed system provides automated candidate evaluation based on predefined technical and communication criteria. Each candidate response is assessed using multiple evaluation dimensions, allowing the platform to generate objective scores, identify strengths and weaknesses, and summarize overall interview performance. Rather than relying solely on keyword matching or simple correctness checks, the evaluation process considers response quality, technical depth, clarity of explanation, ownership of ideas, and communication effectiveness. These evaluations are aggregated into detailed recruiter reports that support informed hiring decisions while reducing the manual effort required from interviewers.
Another important aspect of the proposed system is its emphasis on user experience and conversational realism. The platform incorporates interruption handling, silence detection, transcript refinement, duplicate question prevention, and timing policies to produce smooth and natural conversations. Voice Activity Detection allows the system to recognize when candidates begin and finish speaking, while interruption handling enables candidates to naturally interrupt the interviewer when necessary, creating a more realistic interview environment. Such conversational features distinguish the proposed platform from conventional AI interview systems that often rely on rigid turn-taking mechanisms or predefined interaction patterns.
The implementation of this project utilizes modern software engineering principles and AI technologies. The backend is developed using Python and FastAPI, providing scalable REST APIs and WebSocket communication. Pipecat serves as the real-time voice orchestration framework, while Deepgram provides high-quality Speech-to-Text capabilities, Groq hosts the Llama 3.3 Large Language Model for conversational intelligence and evaluation, and Cartesia generates natural Text-to-Speech output. HTML, CSS, and JavaScript are employed to develop the browser-based user interface, allowing candidates to participate in interviews without requiring specialized software installation. The modular architecture further ensures maintainability, scalability, and future extensibility for integrating additional AI capabilities such as multilingual interviewing, facial behavior analysis, emotion recognition, and cloud deployment.
The significance of this project extends beyond academic research. As organizations increasingly adopt AI-driven recruitment practices, intelligent interview systems have the potential to reduce recruitment costs, improve interview consistency, shorten hiring cycles, and enhance candidate experience. Educational institutions may also utilize such systems to conduct mock interviews, assess student preparedness, and provide personalized feedback before placement activities. Furthermore, the modular design of the proposed platform enables future adaptation for interviews across different technical domains, industries, and organizational requirements.
In conclusion, this project presents a comprehensive AI-powered real-time interviewing platform that integrates advanced speech technologies, conversational AI, adaptive decision-making, and automated evaluation into a unified recruitment solution. By combining real-time voice interaction with intelligent dialogue management and objective performance assessment, the proposed system bridges the gap between conventional online interview platforms and human-conducted technical interviews. The following sections describe the problem addressed by this research, the organization of the project, the underlying technologies, system architecture, implementation methodology, experimental evaluation, and future directions in detail.
1.1.1 Problem Statement
The rapid growth of remote recruitment and online hiring has increased the demand for intelligent interview systems that can efficiently assess candidates while reducing the workload of human interviewers. Traditional interview processes require significant time, resources, and technical expertise, making it difficult for organizations to interview a large number of applicants consistently. Differences in interviewer experience, questioning style, and evaluation criteria may also lead to inconsistent hiring decisions and potential bias.
Although several AI-based interview platforms are available, most existing solutions rely on predefined questionnaires, rule-based conversations, or text-based interactions that do not accurately simulate a real technical interview. These systems often lack the ability to conduct natural voice conversations, generate context-aware follow-up questions, evaluate candidate responses intelligently, and adapt the interview according to candidate performance. As a result, they provide limited assessment of technical knowledge, communication skills, and problem-solving abilities.
Developing an intelligent interview platform also presents several technical challenges, including accurate speech recognition, real-time conversation management, adaptive question generation, response evaluation, and natural speech synthesis. These components must operate together efficiently to provide a smooth and realistic interview experience.
Therefore, there is a need for an AI-driven real-time interview platform capable of conducting adaptive voice-based technical interviews while providing consistent and objective candidate evaluation. The proposed system addresses these challenges by integrating Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), Voice Activity Detection (VAD), Large Language Models (LLMs), Text-to-Speech (TTS), adaptive dialogue management, and automated reporting into a unified framework. The system aims to improve recruitment efficiency, reduce interviewer workload, and provide a realistic and scalable technical interviewing solution.

Chapter 2
Literature Review
In this chapter, a detailed literature review of recent studies related to Artificial Intelligence (AI)-based interview systems is presented to provide an understanding of the latest developments in intelligent recruitment technologies. Researchers have proposed various approaches that integrate Machine Learning (ML), Natural Language Processing (NLP), Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), Large Language Models (LLMs), and conversational AI to automate candidate screening and interviewing processes. These systems aim to reduce recruitment time, improve evaluation consistency, and enhance candidate experience by minimizing manual effort and supporting intelligent decision-making. Recent advancements in speech processing, deep learning, and generative AI have further enabled the development of adaptive interview platforms capable of conducting interactive and context-aware conversations. Furthermore, the increasing demand for remote recruitment has encouraged organizations to adopt intelligent interviewing solutions that provide scalable, consistent, and unbiased candidate evaluation. However, existing solutions still face challenges such as limited adaptability, lack of real-time voice interaction, insufficient contextual understanding, restricted automated evaluation capabilities, dependence on predefined interview workflows, and difficulty handling dynamic interview scenarios. Therefore, reviewing previous research helps identify existing gaps and provides the foundation for developing the proposed AI-Driven Real-Time Interview Platform [1].
The study proposed an AI-powered recruitment framework that integrates Machine Learning techniques to automate candidate screening and preliminary interviews [2]. The system analyzes applicant resumes, compares candidate qualifications with predefined job requirements, and conducts structured interviews using predefined conversational workflows. Machine learning algorithms are employed to rank candidates according to their technical skills, educational background, work experience, and overall suitability for specific job roles. The proposed framework significantly reduces manual effort during the initial stages of recruitment while improving consistency in candidate selection. Experimental results demonstrated improved recruitment efficiency by reducing manual screening effort, increasing consistency in candidate evaluation, and shortening the overall hiring process. The framework also assists recruiters by filtering large numbers of applications within a shorter period. However, the system relies on fixed interview questions and lacks adaptive dialogue management, real-time voice communication, intelligent follow-up questioning, and contextual understanding, limiting its ability to simulate realistic technical interviews and evaluate higher-order problem-solving abilities.
The authors presented a conversational AI interview system based on Large Language Models (LLMs) for conducting intelligent candidate interviews [3]. The proposed system generates context-aware interview questions, maintains conversational flow, and evaluates candidate responses using Natural Language Processing techniques. By understanding previous responses, the system is capable of producing more relevant follow-up questions, thereby creating a more engaging and personalized interview experience. The integration of transformer-based language models enables the interviewer to provide human-like interactions while maintaining coherence throughout the conversation. Experimental evaluation showed improved interaction quality, better candidate engagement, and more natural conversations compared to traditional rule-based chatbot systems. Despite these advantages, the system depends heavily on cloud-based AI services and requires high computational resources, which may increase response latency, operational cost, deployment complexity, and dependency on reliable internet connectivity.
The research introduced an Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)-based interview platform that enables candidates to communicate through natural voice instead of text [4]. Speech input is converted into textual form for further processing and evaluation, providing a more realistic and interactive interview experience. The platform incorporates modern speech recognition algorithms capable of handling continuous voice conversations while reducing the need for manual typing during interviews. Integration with language processing modules enables automatic understanding of candidate responses and supports conversational interaction. Experimental results demonstrated high transcription accuracy under controlled environments and significantly improved user interaction by enabling hands-free communication. Nevertheless, speech recognition performance decreased in noisy environments, for speakers with diverse accents, and during overlapping speech, affecting transcription quality and the overall reliability of candidate evaluation.
The study developed an adaptive dialogue management system capable of generating follow-up interview questions according to candidate responses [5]. The system continuously analyzes conversation context, determines candidate knowledge levels, and dynamically adjusts interview difficulty based on previous answers. This adaptive questioning strategy enables more effective assessment of technical skills while maintaining a natural interview flow throughout the session. The dialogue manager also attempts to prevent abrupt topic transitions and ensures logical progression between interview questions. Experimental results demonstrated improved interview flexibility, enhanced candidate engagement, and better evaluation of technical knowledge when compared to static interview systems. However, maintaining long conversational context, preventing repetitive questioning, handling unexpected candidate responses, and balancing interview coverage across multiple technical topics remained significant challenges during extended interview sessions.
The authors proposed an AI-based candidate assessment system that combines Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Machine Learning algorithms to evaluate candidate responses during interviews [6]. The system analyzes semantic meaning, communication quality, response relevance, grammatical correctness, and linguistic patterns to generate automated performance scores. By applying machine learning models, the framework provides objective and consistent evaluation across multiple candidates while reducing dependence on manual scoring by recruiters. The proposed approach also assists interviewers by identifying strengths and weaknesses in candidate responses through automated analysis. Experimental results indicated improved evaluation consistency, reduced recruiter workload, and faster candidate assessment compared to conventional manual evaluation methods. However, the system primarily focused on textual responses and lacked support for real-time voice interaction, adaptive questioning, emotional understanding, and continuous conversational assessment.
The study presented a multimodal interview analysis framework that integrates speech, facial expressions, and textual responses to assess candidate performance [7]. The proposed system extracts verbal and non-verbal features using deep learning models to evaluate communication skills, confidence, behavioural characteristics, and overall interview performance. By combining multiple sources of information, the framework achieves more comprehensive candidate assessment than systems relying solely on textual responses. Experimental evaluation demonstrated improved prediction accuracy and better understanding of candidate behaviour during interviews. The proposed approach also supports behavioural analysis by incorporating visual cues alongside speech and language processing. However, the framework requires high computational resources, specialized hardware, high-quality video input, and additional processing time, making large-scale real-time deployment more challenging in practical recruitment environments.
The researchers developed a real-time conversational AI platform designed to simulate human interviewers using Large Language Models and speech processing technologies [8]. The platform supports continuous dialogue, generates context-aware follow-up questions, and provides automated feedback based on candidate responses. Integration of speech recognition, language generation, and speech synthesis enables candidates to interact naturally without relying on predefined conversational scripts. The adaptive nature of the platform improves interview realism by allowing conversations to evolve according to candidate performance and response quality. Experimental results showed enhanced interview realism, improved user engagement, and more personalized interview experiences compared to conventional chatbot systems. Despite these advantages, the platform experienced occasional response latency, increased computational requirements, and dependency on stable internet connectivity because most processing was performed using cloud-based AI services.
The study proposed an intelligent recruitment platform that integrates Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), Large Language Models (LLMs), and Text-to-Speech (TTS) technologies to conduct voice-based interviews [9]. The system enables candidates to communicate naturally while automatically evaluating technical knowledge, communication skills, response quality, and overall interview performance. The integration of speech recognition, conversational AI, and speech synthesis creates a complete voice-based interviewing pipeline that closely resembles interviews conducted by human recruiters. The platform also generates automated evaluation reports that assist recruiters during the hiring process. Experimental results demonstrated improved interview automation, reduced recruiter workload, consistent candidate evaluation, and enhanced interview efficiency. However, the proposed system provided limited support for dynamic interview progression, interruption handling, duplicate question prevention, advanced conversational memory, and comprehensive recruiter reporting, leaving opportunities for further research and improvement.

Chapter 3
System Background
This chapter presents the technical background and core technologies that form the foundation of the proposed AI-Driven Real-Time Interview Platform. Unlike conventional AI interview systems that primarily rely on text-based interactions, the proposed platform integrates real-time speech processing, intelligent dialogue management, adaptive question generation, and automated candidate evaluation into a unified architecture. The objective is to simulate a realistic technical interview by enabling natural voice-based communication while continuously analyzing candidate responses.
The proposed system combines several modern Artificial Intelligence technologies, including Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), Large Language Models (LLMs), Text-to-Speech (TTS), Voice Activity Detection (VAD), WebSocket-based real-time communication, and intelligent evaluation algorithms. These components work together to provide a seamless interview experience in which the interviewer can dynamically generate questions, interrupt naturally, evaluate candidate responses, and produce comprehensive interview reports.
This chapter discusses the fundamental technologies, system architecture, speech processing pipeline, dialogue management process, evaluation methodology, and performance considerations employed in the proposed platform. It also describes the role of each major software component and explains how these technologies collectively contribute to building a scalable and intelligent AI interviewer.
3.1 Overall System Architecture
The proposed AI Interview Platform follows a modular client-server architecture designed to support low-latency communication and real-time interview interaction. The system is divided into several independent components responsible for speech recognition, dialogue management, candidate evaluation, and speech synthesis. Each module communicates through asynchronous APIs and WebSocket connections, allowing simultaneous processing of multiple interview sessions.
The interview process begins when a candidate joins the interview through a web browser. Audio captured from the user's microphone is transmitted continuously to the backend server using WebRTC/WebSocket communication. The backend processes the incoming audio using a Voice Activity Detection (VAD) module to determine speech boundaries before forwarding speech segments to the Speech-to-Text engine. The transcribed text is then analyzed by the Dialogue Manager, which coordinates interview flow, candidate evaluation, follow-up question generation, and conversation state management. Finally, the generated interviewer response is converted into natural speech using the Text-to-Speech engine and streamed back to the candidate.
This modular architecture enables each subsystem to operate independently while maintaining synchronized communication throughout the interview session. Such separation improves scalability, simplifies maintenance, and allows individual AI components to be upgraded without affecting the remaining modules.

3.1.1 Frontend Layer
The frontend serves as the primary interface between the candidate and the AI interviewer. It is developed using modern web technologies, including HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, to provide a responsive and interactive interview environment. The frontend is responsible for capturing microphone input, displaying interview questions, presenting conversation history, and streaming synthesized audio responses generated by the backend.
To achieve real-time interaction, the frontend establishes a persistent WebSocket connection with the FastAPI server. Unlike conventional HTTP communication, WebSockets support bidirectional communication, enabling continuous transmission of audio streams and interview events with minimal latency.
The frontend also manages interview controls such as starting or ending the interview session, displaying interview status, monitoring microphone activity, and presenting evaluation reports upon completion of the interview.

3.2 Speech Processing Pipeline
Speech processing is one of the most critical components of the proposed AI-Driven Real-Time Interview Platform. Unlike traditional chatbot systems that rely solely on textual communication, the proposed platform supports continuous voice interaction between the interviewer and the candidate. The speech processing pipeline transforms spoken language into machine-readable text, enables intelligent processing of candidate responses, and converts generated interview questions back into natural-sounding speech.
The pipeline consists of three major components: Voice Activity Detection (VAD), Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), and Text-to-Speech (TTS). These components operate sequentially while maintaining real-time communication throughout the interview session. By combining these technologies, the system achieves low-latency interaction and provides an interview experience that closely resembles communication with a human interviewer.
3.2.1 Voice Activity Detection (VAD)
Voice Activity Detection (VAD) is responsible for determining whether the incoming audio stream contains human speech or silence. Since microphone input contains pauses, breathing sounds, background noise, and environmental disturbances, processing every audio frame would significantly increase computational overhead and response latency. The VAD module filters non-speech segments before forwarding meaningful audio to the speech recognition engine.
The proposed platform employs the Silero Voice Activity Detection model, which continuously analyzes incoming audio frames and determines whether speech is present. Once speech is detected, the corresponding audio segment is transmitted to the Speech-to-Text module. When silence is detected for a predefined duration, the current speech segment is finalized and processed.
The incorporation of VAD significantly reduces unnecessary computation while improving transcription quality by preventing fragmented speech recognition. Additionally, it enables natural conversational behavior by allowing the AI interviewer to distinguish between intentional pauses and the completion of candidate responses.

3.3 Dialogue Management
Dialogue management is one of the fundamental components of the proposed AI-Driven Real-Time Interview Platform, as it governs the interaction between the AI interviewer and the candidate throughout the interview session. Unlike conventional chatbot systems that follow predefined conversation flows, the proposed platform employs an intelligent dialogue management mechanism capable of maintaining conversational context, generating adaptive interview questions, evaluating candidate responses, and deciding appropriate follow-up actions in real time.
The dialogue manager acts as the central controller of the interview pipeline by coordinating communication between the Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) module, the Large Language Model (LLM), the evaluation engine, and the Text-to-Speech (TTS) module. Once a candidate's spoken response has been converted into text by the Speech-to-Text module, the dialogue manager analyzes the response together with the current interview state before determining the next action. Depending on the quality, completeness, and relevance of the candidate's answer, the system may generate a new interview question, ask a probing follow-up question, request clarification, or proceed to the next interview topic.
Unlike rule-based interview systems, the proposed dialogue manager maintains contextual awareness throughout the interview. Every candidate response is stored as part of the conversation history, enabling the system to reference previous answers and avoid repetitive questioning. This contextual memory allows the interviewer to conduct a coherent conversation that closely resembles the behavior of a human interviewer.
The dialogue management process also incorporates interview control mechanisms such as session initialization, candidate introduction, topic progression, interruption handling, silence detection, interview termination, and final report generation. These mechanisms ensure that the interview proceeds smoothly while maintaining a structured assessment process.

The first set of improvements focused on conversation management.
Major additions included:
Semantic duplicate question detection 
MetaConversationGuard 
Three-stage "I Don't Know" handling 
Domain synchronization 
Improved interview progression 
The duplicate question detection module prevents the interviewer from asking semantically similar questions multiple times. MetaConversationGuard processes conversational commands such as "repeat the question" or "next question" without affecting candidate evaluation. The IDK Guard applies a three-stage policy consisting of question rephrasing, contextual hints, and domain skipping, producing a more natural interview experience.
Phase 6B – Transcript Quality and Fairness
Live speech transcription frequently contains repeated words, sentence fragments, and recognition noise. To address this issue, transcript preprocessing was introduced before answer evaluation.
Major improvements include:
Stutter removal 
N-gram repetition removal 
Progressive phrase collapsing 
Transcript quality scoring 
Fairness-aware rubric adjustment 
The transcript quality module calculates repeated token ratios and reduction statistics to determine whether speech recognition quality is sufficiently reliable. When excessive transcription noise is detected, communication-related evaluation weights are automatically reduced while technical understanding receives greater emphasis. This approach minimizes unfair penalties caused by imperfect speech recognition.
Phase 6C – Reporting Improvements
The final reporting system produces significantly more informative interview reports than earlier project versions.
The generated report now includes:
Technical score profile

Interview completion percentage

Recruiter hiring recommendation

Chapter 7
Conclusion
This Final Year Project aimed to design and implement an AI-driven real-time interview platform capable of conducting adaptive technical interviews through natural voice interaction while providing structured candidate evaluation and detailed recruiter-oriented reports. The motivation behind this project was to address the limitations of conventional online assessment systems, which often rely on static questionnaires, limited interaction, and manual evaluation. By integrating modern artificial intelligence technologies with real-time speech processing, the developed system offers a more realistic interview experience that closely resembles interactions with a human interviewer.
The final implementation successfully achieves the primary objectives defined at the beginning of the project. The developed platform is capable of conducting complete voice-based interviews, dynamically adapting questions according to candidate responses, evaluating answers using a structured multi-dimensional rubric, and generating comprehensive interview reports. Unlike traditional chatbot-based interview systems, the platform maintains conversational flow, tracks interview coverage across multiple technical domains, and handles interruptions and conversational exceptions using a dedicated guard pipeline.
The completed system integrates several state-of-the-art technologies into a unified architecture. FastAPI serves as the backend API framework responsible for session management and report generation, while Pipecat provides the real-time voice orchestration framework connecting speech recognition, dialogue management, and speech synthesis. Deepgram Nova-2 performs streaming speech-to-text transcription, Groq Llama 3.3 70B Versatile is used as the sole large language model for question generation and candidate evaluation, and Cartesia produces low-latency interviewer speech through streaming text-to-speech synthesis. Together, these components enable a responsive and production-oriented voice interview pipeline.
One of the most significant achievements of this project is the implementation of a structured adaptive dialogue system. Rather than asking questions sequentially from a static question bank, the system evaluates each candidate response before deciding whether to probe deeper into the current topic or advance to another technical domain. This behaviour is coordinated through the DialogueManager, DecisionEngine, and CoverageEngine, enabling interviews to progress naturally while ensuring balanced assessment across the predefined Junior AI Engineer blueprint domains.
Another major contribution is the introduction of a comprehensive guard pipeline that improves interview robustness during live conversations. Guards were developed to handle situations such as repeated questions, conversational meta-commands, candidate uncertainty ("I don't know"), duplicate speech recognition output, and transcript noise. These mechanisms significantly improve interview quality by preventing unnecessary scoring errors while maintaining conversational continuity. The addition of transcript quality assessment and fairness-aware score adjustment further ensures that candidates are not unfairly penalized because of speech recognition inaccuracies or microphone-related issues.
The reporting subsystem also represents a substantial enhancement over many existing interview simulators. Instead of generating only an overall interview score, the developed platform produces structured JSON reports containing communication and technical score profiles, recruiter-oriented hiring recommendations, adaptive interview traces, domain assessment maps, interview completion statistics, and documented limitations. This reporting approach increases transparency and makes interview outcomes easier for recruiters and researchers to interpret.
Extensive testing was performed throughout the development process. More than forty automated regression tests validate the functionality of session management, adaptive dialogue flow, evaluation logic, transcript processing, reporting modules, and voice integration components. In addition to automated testing, multiple live interview sessions were conducted using the complete voice pipeline to evaluate speech recognition quality, latency, interruption handling, transcript generation, and overall user experience. These evaluations confirmed that the implemented architecture functions reliably under realistic interview conditions while maintaining stable conversational behaviour.
Although the developed platform successfully fulfills the primary objectives of the project, several limitations remain. Speech recognition quality continues to depend on microphone hardware, background noise, internet connectivity, and cloud-based transcription services. The current implementation supports only a single active voice client because of the selected Pipecat transport architecture. Furthermore, advanced multimodal analysis proposed during the initial project planning—including facial expression recognition and speech emotion recognition—was intentionally deferred to maintain a manageable project scope and prioritize a stable end-to-end voice interviewing system.
The project also demonstrates that practical engineering decisions sometimes provide greater value than pursuing overly ambitious research objectives. During development, several planned components—including full Item Response Theory (IRT), Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT), TensorFlow-based emotion recognition, and webcam behavioural analysis—were replaced by more practical alternatives such as blueprint-driven adaptive questioning, structured LLM evaluation, transcript fairness adjustment, and robust dialogue management. These decisions resulted in a more reliable, maintainable, and demonstrable system while remaining academically defensible.
Future enhancements can significantly extend the capabilities of the platform. Integrating multimodal emotion recognition using speech and facial analysis would provide richer behavioural insights during interviews. Implementing WebRTC transport could reduce communication latency compared to raw WebSocket audio streaming. A production deployment architecture using Docker, Kubernetes, or cloud infrastructure would improve scalability and enable concurrent interview sessions. Additional work may also include expanding the role registry beyond Junior AI Engineer positions, incorporating multilingual interview support, introducing real-time LLM streaming responses, and validating the evaluation methodology through large-scale human studies using the prepared Cohen's κ export framework.
From an academic perspective, the project demonstrates that large language models can be integrated effectively into structured assessment systems when combined with deterministic evaluation logic, adaptive dialogue management, transparent reporting, and clearly documented limitations. Rather than replacing traditional assessment entirely, the developed platform illustrates how AI can augment technical interviewing by improving consistency, reducing manual effort, and providing standardized evaluation criteria while maintaining human oversight.
In conclusion, this Final Year Project successfully delivers a complete AI-driven real-time interview platform that combines adaptive questioning, voice interaction, structured evaluation, and comprehensive reporting into a unified system. Through six iterative development phases and multiple rounds of live testing, the project evolved from an initial prototype into a robust and extensible interview framework suitable for academic demonstration and future research. The completed system establishes a strong foundation upon which future work in multimodal assessment, scalable deployment, and advanced adaptive interviewing can be built, while demonstrating the practical potential of artificial intelligence to improve modern technical recruitment processes.

A.8 Development Challenges
Several practical challenges were encountered throughout the implementation.
Voice Processing
Real-time browser microphones produced inconsistent audio quality, requiring extensive tuning of Voice Activity Detection parameters.
Transcript Fragmentation
Streaming Speech-to-Text frequently generated overlapping or duplicated transcript fragments. Phase 6B introduced transcript cleanup utilities to address these issues.
Adaptive Interview Flow
Repeated questions occasionally occurred during probing. Semantic question deduplication was implemented to prevent redundant questioning.
Meta Conversation
Users often responded with phrases such as "next question" or "I don't know." Dedicated conversational guards were implemented to distinguish these responses from technical answers.
Fair Evaluation
Candidates with noisy microphones were unfairly penalized in communication scoring. A transcript-quality assessment module was introduced to dynamically adjust communication weights when excessive speech noise was detected.
Live Voice Reliability
Initial voice testing revealed issues including interrupted speech, reconnect failures, missed speech detection, and aggressive Voice Activity Detection. These were resolved through the Voice Hotfix following Phase 6.
